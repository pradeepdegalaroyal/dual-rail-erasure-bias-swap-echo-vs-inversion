# Research gap

## State of the art (from the supplied literature)

Superconducting-circuit quantum computing is organised around a small set of engineering
quantities: qubit design, noise (energy relaxation, dephasing), control and readout
(Krantz et al., 2019, doi:10.1063/1.5089550). Fault-tolerant architectures built on such circuits
increasingly convert the dominant physical error, photon or excitation loss, into a *flagged
erasure*, which is far cheaper to correct than an unflagged error. A flagged qubit is simply
discarded or marked, so the accepted shots look error free.

Three results from the supplied set describe exactly the physics that this accepted-shot picture
leaves out:

* Dissipation makes the effective Hamiltonian non-Hermitian. Gong et al. (2018,
  doi:10.1103/PhysRevX.8.031079) classify non-Hermitian phases and identify a topological index for
  completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) quantum channels. When one conditions on "no jump" the dynamics
  is generated by a non-Hermitian operator, and conditioning is what erasure postselection does.
* Periodic driving defines an effective, time-averaged Hamiltonian (Floquet engineering; Oka and
  Kitamura, 2019, doi:10.1146/annurev-conmatphys-031218-013423). A periodic exchange of two modes
  therefore averages their *loss rates* as well as their frequencies.
* Quasi-static disorder is the generic dephasing mechanism that echoes remove and that interacting
  systems turn into slow dynamics (Abanin et al., 2019, doi:10.1103/RevModPhys.91.021001).

## Gap

The reviews state that quantum devices are limited by materials and fabrication variation, and the
landmark work establishes the non-Hermitian and Floquet tools. What is missing is a *quantitative*
statement of what erasure postselection leaves behind when the two modes of an erasure qubit do not
decay at the same rate. Mode-resolved loss produces an erasure-invisible conditional error: the
accepted state is pulled toward the longer-lived mode. This term is second order in the
infidelity of the state, so it is usually dropped, but it is the term that grows with hold time and
with device asymmetry. Two mitigations are available and have not been compared head to head:
a hardware one (a Floquet-style periodic mode exchange, which averages the loss rates) and a
classical one (inverting the known no-jump map in post-processing). Their break-even depends on
pulse error and on how far the loss asymmetry drifts between calibration and use, a quantity that
is not studied in the supplied literature.

**Novelty (one sentence).** This project gives, in simulation, a closed-form and numerically
verified scaling for the erasure-invisible non-Hermitian bias of a dual-rail memory and maps the
regime in which a periodic swap echo beats calibrated classical inversion of that bias.

## Hypotheses and success metrics

* **H1 (scaling).** After erasure postselection with symmetric dephasing set to zero, the mean
  conditional infidelity over the six Pauli inputs equals (2 eta T)^2 / 24, where eta is the
  loss asymmetry and T the hold time in mean lifetimes. Falsified if the log-log slope against
  eta differs from 2 by more than 0.1 or the deviation from the formula exceeds 10 percent.
* **H2 (echo).** A two-pulse swap echo removes the bias exactly for ideal pulses, and beats bare
  postselection only if the pulse over-rotation is below a boundary that depends on hold time.
  Success metric: the sign of log10(infidelity echo / infidelity bare) over the (asymmetry, hold)
  and (over-rotation, hold) planes.
* **H3 (echo versus inversion).** With a fresh calibration, classical inversion matches or beats the
  echo; the echo wins only when the asymmetry drifts between calibration and use by more than a
  threshold and the pulses are accurate. Success metric: shot-limited Bloch-vector mean squared
  error (MSE), 5 seeds, mean and standard deviation.

## Scale and feasibility

Everything is simulated exactly with a nine-dimensional Lindblad superoperator for the three-level
single-excitation manifold (empty, mode a, mode b); no hardware, no variational training, and the
whole study runs in under two minutes on a laptop CPU. Results are statements about a model, with
dimensionless rates in units of the mean lifetime, not about any specific device.
